Citizen Jury Simulation Project

Summary of the Citizen Jury Simulator

The Citizen Jury Simulator Project was created to allow citizens to experience the procedures involved in criminal accusations and legal disputes. The purpose of this project is to improve public understanding of legal processes and to help individuals become more aware of their rights and responsibilities. By simulating situations similar to real-world cases, the project also aims to strengthen users’ ability to respond wisely to legal conflicts and complicated social situations.




Project Intention

At some point in life, anyone may unexpectedly encounter criminal accusations or legal disputes. If we do not understand legal procedures or lack experience, we may find ourselves placed in unfair situations or even receive unjust outcomes.

Through this realization, I came to believe that a simulator project capable of providing indirect yet meaningful experiences could become highly valuable for many people today. By allowing users to experience situations they may never have faced before, this project seeks to help individuals prepare themselves for uncertain situations in the future.

For this reason, I attempted to create an AI-based platform in the form of a game-like simulation where users can experience virtual criminal accusations and legal procedures. Through indirect experiences within the simulator, users may gain insight and preparedness that could help them avoid unjust situations in real life. The core value of this simulator lies in the importance of experience itself, which is structured through rules, participation, evidence, and judgment.

The intention behind this project is not to trivialize serious situations, but rather to encourage users to approach difficult subjects with both openness and sincerity. By generating AI-based virtual cases, users can indirectly experience the complexity of legal and social judgment in an interactive environment. Because this project places strong emphasis on evidence and reasoning, the support of AI platforms plays an essential role throughout the experience.

Users may initiate or suspend prosecutions through the criminal complaint and legal process system. Once a prosecution is initiated, participants may gather and submit evidence on their own. Evidence materials can be uploaded to threads where users with similar perspectives may exchange opinions, share information, and collaborate.

If a participant provides persuasive and well-reasoned evidence, they may become a “leader” and participate in the final defense vote.

During the final defense stage, even participants in disadvantageous positions may attempt one last argument if they possess convincing and rational evidence. Leaders are given the choice of whether or not to use this final opportunity depending on the situation. Other participants may observe whether the leader decides to make a final defense or remain silent.

Through experiences aligned with the project’s intention, users may begin to understand not only the perspectives of victims or defendants, but also the responsibilities and dilemmas faced by legal advocates. Because the simulator is designed with a high degree of freedom and game-like interaction, various unexpected events and situations may emerge naturally during participation.

Ultimately, this project may be understood as a virtual citizen jury simulation “game” in which participants may take on various roles: A person initiating prosecution / A person attempting to suspend prosecution / A participant submitting evidence / A participant beginning the final defense / A voter deciding guilt or innocence in a virtual case / Or an individual experiencing the perspectives of both victims and defendants through the final verdict Participants are welcome to approach the project either casually or seriously. In the end, the simulator operates through a rule-based system where wise and persuasive evidence becomes the foundation of the final defense.

It is my hope that everyone who participates in this “game” will gain valuable experiences and develop a deeper understanding of the perspectives of others through indirect participation and interaction.




Project Introduction

This project aims to create a Citizen Jury Simulator website with the assistance of the AI platform ChatGPT. By generating virtual cases and structuring branching, condition-based scenarios, the platform seeks to encourage user participation within an operator-driven interactive simulation environment.

One of the project’s strongest aspects is its stage-based trial progression system built around voting mechanics, providing users with an interactive simulation web service that reflects the flow of legal procedures.

The project becomes especially compelling because users are able to experience the “process” itself: How criminal accusations proceed / Why evidence is important / How public opinion and legal judgment may conflict / How emotional judgment differs from legal reasoning

To briefly introduce the storytelling structure: An operator presents a case and begins a vote regarding whether prosecution should proceed or be suspended. Depending on the voting ratio, the trial may officially begin. Participants then collect evidence related to the case and upload materials to their own threads.

As the conflict between prosecution and suspension continues, users vote on the most persuasive argument, leading to the beginning of the final defense stage. At the conclusion, the operator — acting as the judge — delivers the final verdict.

When the result of the verdict is delivered through notifications or email, the trial for that particular case comes to an end.

After each case concludes, both participants and operators may write reviews. Based on these reviews, a decision may be made regarding whether a retrial should occur. The creator of the project may then hold an additional vote through the website structure to determine whether the retrial process will proceed.

Furthermore, because the platform could appear similar to a real legal system, it is important to clarify that the project is entirely based on AI-generated virtual cases created through the ChatGPT platform. This distinction exists to prevent misuse and to ensure that the project is understood as a fictional simulation experience rather than an actual legal service.

Identity of the Website

This website is an interactive experiential platform created as both a research archive and a digital exhibition space. At the same time, it functions as a personal brand portfolio website.

The platform is designed around two types of users: operators and guests.

When visitors first enter the website, I wanted them to feel: “This looks interesting.” / “I want to participate.” / “This could become a meaningful experience.”

For this reason, I aimed to create a website that combines immersion, professionalism, and interactive storytelling.

I wanted the rules of the game and the platform’s high degree of freedom to become attractive experiences for users visiting the website. Through participation-driven systems and interactive structures, I hoped to encourage users to remain engaged for longer periods of time while increasing their curiosity and anticipation regarding the unfolding events within the simulator.
